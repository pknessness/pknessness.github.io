Metal Wii Remote: Oct 16, 2025

This project began in 2023 when I first modded my Wii and was getting controllers off ebay, and thought it would be cool to make a shell for a controller out of metal. My motivation and progress on this project waxed and waned very much over the two years. On this note, I went to my local makerspace and tried to get a 3D model of an existing wii shell so that I wouldn't have to CAD it myself, as I'm generally not a big fan of doing complex CAD, but the results were definitely less than ideal.

I had also previously looked online for wii remote shell cad, but also no luck there. In the end, I thought, ok I'll just design the shell myself, and began some light CAD work trying to get the PCB to mount into the shell. Before I had gotten too far into this process, I had the brilliant idea that I could make this process easier for myself if I also designed the pcb from scratch, and that would require me to also do all the firmware from scratch, and at the time that seemed like an appealing idea to me, and thus this project began anew.

Once I had decided to make the software from scratch, I thought it would be best to actually begin with that, since that would be the most difficult part of this by far, and also the part that I could do without the need of a 3D printer, (which I do not have of my own, and I was using the makerspace for iteration). The first order of business was to look into the protocol of the wii remote itself. The wii has a dedicated fanbase to this day (of which I am one), and they already have a wonderful page on the protocol of a wiimote. It seemed to be a pretty simple setup, which is that its a mostly standard HID bluetooth classic device.

The next order of business is figuring out how exactly HID over bluetooth works. Some resources I found:
Classic Bluetooth & Bluetooth low energy - what's the difference?
Bluetooth Classic & BLE with ESP32
Codenomicon Technical Deep Dive: Bluetooth L2CAP
During my research I also found someone who wrote code that did the opposite of what I wanted it to do, which is connect a Wii Remote to an ESP32. However, connecting to a wiimote was a lot less difficult than connecting to a wii, assumedly because Nintendo was more concerned about clone wiimotes than someone reusing their Wiimote on their laptop. From there, my goal was to use examples from ESP-idf to start off and then modify the specifics to match the Wiimote specs I found on wiibrew.

Next on the docket was to get something working. I began initially with bluedroid, as it had examples with bluetooth mice/keyboards I thought I could modify. I rotated around a few examples, but ended up settling on the esp_hid_device example, as it was the most barebones. To my knowledge at the moment, there are a few requirements for a wiimote to connect to the wii: The MAC address's first three has to be one of the nintendo specific ones, the SDP record has to be correct, the product and vendor id have to be correct, and it has to use legacy pairing with the correct pin.

I got the bluedroid example to pair using dolphin emulator, but no reaction on the wii. I hadn't yet implemented the SDP record yet, because bluedroid's SDP record api is pretty strict and not very flexible. I would probably have to modify bluedroid to be able to do it, so I briefly looked at an alternative bluetooth stack, btstack. btstack had a lot more flexibilty with SDP, but unfortunately btstack doesn't support legacy pairing.
